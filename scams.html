Job Scams

Job scams involve fake employment opportunities created to trick people into paying fees, sharing personal information, or completing tasks without receiving promised payment. Scammers may advertise high salaries, easy work, or guaranteed hiring to attract job seekers. They sometimes impersonate legitimate companies to appear trustworthy. These scams can cause financial loss and expose sensitive information, which may lead to identity theft. To protect yourself, research the company, verify job offers through official websites, check the recruiter's identity, and avoid employers who demand upfront payments or promise unrealistic earnings.

Tech Support Scams

Tech support scams occur when scammers pretend to be computer technicians or customer service representatives and claim that a device has a problem. They may use fake error messages, alarming pop-ups, or unexpected calls to convince users that their computers are infected or at risk. Scammers may then ask for payment, personal information, or remote access to the device, which can expose private files and accounts. To protect yourself, avoid clicking suspicious pop-ups, never give unknown individuals remote access to your device, and contact official support services if you need assistance.

Banking Scams

Banking scams involve fake calls, text messages, or emails that impersonate banks to steal account details, passwords, PINs, and one-time passwords (OTPs). Scammers may claim that your account has suspicious activity, is temporarily blocked, or requires immediate verification to pressure you into sharing sensitive information. These scams can result in stolen funds, unauthorized transactions, and loss of account access. To avoid banking scams, never share your passwords or verification codes, avoid clicking suspicious links, and contact your bank directly through its official website, app, or verified hotline whenever you receive an unusual message.

Lottery Scams

Lottery scams involve fake announcements claiming that someone has won a cash prize, lottery, or special giveaway, even if they never entered a contest. Scammers often pretend to represent legitimate organizations and ask winners to pay processing fees, taxes, or other charges before receiving their supposed rewards. They may also request personal information or banking details to make their scheme appear legitimate. To avoid lottery scams, verify prize announcements through official sources, and never send money or reveal sensitive information to claim a prize you cannot confirm.

Subscription Scams

Subscription scams use misleading advertisements, websites, or offers to trick users into signing up for services with hidden charges or unwanted recurring payments. Some scammers promote free trials but fail to clearly explain when payments begin or how subscriptions can be canceled. Others may use fake renewal notices to trick users into clicking suspicious links or revealing payment details. To avoid subscription scams, read the terms and conditions carefully before signing up, verify the service provider, check renewal dates, and regularly review your bank statements for unfamiliar charges.

Impersonation Scams

Impersonation scams happen when criminals pretend to be family members, friends, government officials, or company representatives to deceive people. They may use fake accounts, stolen photos, or urgent messages claiming that someone needs money or that an account requires immediate attention. These tactics can pressure victims into sending money or revealing confidential information. To avoid impersonation scams, confirm unusual requests directly with the person or organization through a trusted contact method, check account details carefully, and never act hastily because of an urgent message.
